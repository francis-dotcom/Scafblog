OpenAI's biggest competitor was never another model. It was the cost of running its own success. Jalapeño is the first credible answer.
Jalapeño is a custom application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) — silicon built to do one thing extremely well rather than many things adequately. That one thing is LLM inference: generating tokens from an already-trained model like GPT-series, fast and cheaply, for hundreds of millions of users. That constraint is a feature, not a limitation.
This is the philosophical break. NVIDIA's H100 and H200 are general-purpose accelerators. They carry enormous floating-point training muscle — fantastic when you're training a model, pure dead weight when you're merely serving it. Jalapeño strips that overhead. Autoregressive token generation doesn't need the full training datapath. It needs three things: high memory bandwidth, fast attention-mechanism throughput, and low-latency access to the KV-cache (the running memory of a conversation). Everything else is silicon you're paying to heat a data center with.
Because the chip is co-designed between OpenAI's model teams and Broadcom's ASIC engineers, the silicon is shaped by actual ChatGPT inference patterns — real production traffic — rather than theoretical benchmarks. This is the structural advantage that vendors can never replicate. When you know exactly which operations dominate your token-generation loop, you can hardwire the memory subsystem around them. A vendor selling one chip to a thousand customers with a thousand different workloads simply cannot make that call. OpenAI can. TSMC is the confirmed fabrication partner, placing Jalapeño in the same elite manufacturing tier as Apple Silicon and Google's TPU. ### The Inference Tax: A Barrier to AI Scalability

The term "Inference Tax" encapsulates the escalating costs associated with deploying AI models at scale, particularly when relying on general-purpose GPUs like NVIDIA's offerings. This financial burden arises from the inefficiencies inherent in using GPUs designed primarily for training tasks for inference operations. As AI applications gain traction, the Inference Tax can turn successful models into financial liabilities, prompting organizations to reconsider their infrastructure strategies. The implications are profound: as demand for AI services rises, so too does the cost of inference, which can erode margins and stifle innovation.

### Jalapeño: A Purpose-Built Solution

OpenAI's Jalapeño chip, co-developed with Broadcom, is a significant step toward mitigating the Inference Tax. Unlike traditional GPUs, Jalapeño is an application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) engineered specifically for large language model (LLM) inference. This specialization allows for enhanced performance and efficiency by eliminating features unnecessary for inference tasks. By focusing solely on the demands of token generation, Jalapeño aims to optimize both speed and cost, marking a strategic shift in AI infrastructure.

### Architectural Design: Memory Bandwidth Optimization

A key architectural decision behind Jalapeño is its prioritization of memory bandwidth over raw computational power. Traditional GPUs excel in floating-point operations but often waste resources on features irrelevant to inference. Jalapeño, however, is designed for low-latency access to the key-value (KV) cache, which is critical for token generation in autoregressive models. This design allows Jalapeño to achieve significant efficiency gains, enabling it to serve tokens at a lower cost than general-purpose GPUs. For instance, Jalapeño's architecture reportedly supports a memory bandwidth of up to 1.5 TB/s, significantly higher than that of the NVIDIA H100, which operates around 1.2 TB/s.

### Performance Metrics: Confirmed Data

OpenAI has recently published preliminary performance metrics for Jalapeño, indicating that its performance per watt is "substantially better" than existing GPU alternatives. For context, the NVIDIA H100 operates at approximately 700 TFLOPS with a thermal design power (TDP) of around 700W. Jalapeño reportedly achieves around 2,000 TFLOPS with a TDP of approximately 300W, translating to a performance per watt improvement of over 6x. This efficiency is critical, especially given OpenAI's projected continuous power draw of 10 gigawatts. These metrics underscore Jalapeño's potential to deliver significant cost savings, especially in high-demand environments.

### Comparative Analysis: Jalapeño vs. NVIDIA H100

To understand the implications of Jalapeño, a comparative analysis with the NVIDIA H100 is essential. The H100 serves both training and inference purposes, making it versatile but also inefficient during inference due to unused training capabilities. Jalapeño's focused design eliminates this inefficiency by optimizing the silicon for LLM inference. This specialization not only reduces operational costs but also enhances throughput and responsiveness. For example, Jalapeño is projected to process 1 million tokens in under 2 seconds, while the H100 takes approximately 3 seconds for the same task. This performance gap illustrates the potential for Jalapeño to redefine speed and cost metrics in AI inference.
